From STL to a print-ready plan

A careful slicer workflow.

Use this checklist to inspect a LuminaPrint export in Cura, PrusaSlicer, Bambu Studio, or another slicer compatible with STL files. Exact settings depend on your printer, material, and design.

Export and import the STL

Download the model from the design studio, then open or import the STL in your slicer. Keep the original file so you can return to it if you change the design.

Check orientation and scale

Confirm the model is upright, sits on the build plate as intended, and has the expected dimensions. Do not rely on automatic scaling without checking the displayed measurements.

Inspect the model

Review the model from several angles and use your slicer's repair or mesh analysis tools if it reports errors. Examine thin walls, openings, rims, and mounting features closely.

Choose printer-specific settings

Start with a profile you already trust for your printer and material. A 0.4 mm nozzle is a common starting point; choose wall count, layer height, and temperatures based on the filament and the shade's wall thickness.

Preview every layer

Slice the model, then inspect the layer preview for gaps, unsupported regions, unexpected infill, or missing walls. Supports and vase mode depend on the geometry and selected design mode—verify the preview before printing.

Print safely

Use a suitable 3D-printing filament and follow its manufacturer's guidance. Printed plastic can soften or ignite near hot bulbs: use a cool-running LED, keep it clear of the shade, and never leave a lit print unattended.

Important: These are general inspection steps, not universal print settings or a guarantee that a model is compatible with every printer. Follow your printer and filament manufacturers' instructions.
