Helpful answers

Questions, clearly answered.

A quick reference for getting started, understanding the design terms, and taking an exported model to your slicer.

What is LuminaPrint 3D?

A browser-based parametric design studio for creating custom lamp shades and lanterns and exporting their geometry as 3D models.

Do I need to install software?

The design studio runs in a modern browser. A separate slicer and 3D printer are needed for preparing and making a physical part.

Do I need previous CAD experience?

No. The studio is based on visual controls for shaping the profile and changing design parameters. Familiarity with 3D printing can help when preparing the export.

What is a Bézier profile?

It is a smooth 2D curve adjusted with control points. In LuminaPrint, the curve defines the outline that becomes the lamp shade's overall shape.

What are procedural patterns?

They are repeating surfaces generated from design rules rather than sculpted by hand. LuminaPrint includes sinusoidal ribs, origami knurling, and pleated fins.

What is a Voronoi pattern?

Voronoi geometry divides a surface into irregular neighboring cells. LuminaPrint can use these cells as openings around a cylindrical shade.

Can I customize my shade dimensions?

Yes. The studio provides controls for height, top and bottom radii, wall thickness, and pattern settings within the supported ranges.

What is an STL file?

STL is a common format that represents a 3D surface as triangles. Slicer software can use it to prepare a model for a specific printer.

Which slicers can I use?

The exported STL can be opened in compatible slicers, including Cura, PrusaSlicer, and Bambu Studio. Confirm the resulting model and settings in your slicer.

Can I print an exported design directly?

The export is intended for 3D-printing workflows, but it is not a guarantee of success on every printer. Inspect the model in your slicer and choose suitable material, settings, and LED hardware.
